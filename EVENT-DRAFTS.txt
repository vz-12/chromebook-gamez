VOIDRUNNER — ALL HALLOWS
Book I: THE LOST SOUL
Drafted 27 Sep 2026. Nothing here is built. Prepatch only; never deployed.


================================================================================
0. THE FRAME
================================================================================

ALL HALLOWS is the yearly October event. Each year is a BOOK: the same place,
the same guide, and the same systems, with one new chapter of the storyline.
This file is Book I.

THE PREMISE, in one fact the game already has:
  Quitting a run from the menu files it: quitToMenu() calls gameOver(), and
  it gets a score, a place on the board and a line in the ledger. Closing the
  tab mid-run does none of that. The run is never written down. It simply
  stops where it was, and it stays there.

  A LOST SOUL is one of those runs. Its tab was closed, and nothing came to
  take it anywhere.

THE THEME: things that never end properly. All three bosses are people, in
the game's usual way, and each one is stuck on not finishing:
  THE BACKLOG     never starts what he has   ("I'll get to it.")
  DEAD GAME       never leaves               ("Still here, though.")
  THE RAGE QUIT   never lets anything end    ("That one didn't count.")
The soul is what they leave behind, and the event is the soul getting
finished.

THE HALLOWEEN TIE: the jack-o'-lantern legend IS a lost-soul story (Stingy
Jack, refused everywhere, wandering with a coal in a carved lantern). So the
soul carries a small carved lantern once it has one, the event skins are
lanterns, and the pumpkin iconography belongs to the story rather than being
pasted over it.


================================================================================
1. THE PEOPLE
================================================================================

THE OUTSIDER (existing; the guide)
  Voice as written in HUB_TUT_STEPS: flat, singular and unconditional. He
  tells you what a thing costs and never says you are welcome. He left the
  crowd himself, which matters by the end: the soul was LEFT, and he LEFT.
  He is the only one who does not change over the event.

THE LOST SOUL (new)
  A run with its tab closed at WAVE 19, HULL 4%. It flew the ordinary starting
  hull, the one everyone begins in. It does not know who it was flying for, only
  that someone said he would be right back.
  Its voice starts as fragments, lowercase, trailing off, like a paused
  frame. It gets more whole with each ascension:
    start          "...nineteen."
    after I        lowercase, but whole sentences
    after II       capitals, full sentences; its own voice
    after III      plain, and sure of itself
  How it looks over the event (the user draws it):
    0  a small cold light on the floor, beside one unlit candle
    Q1 a hull outline gathers round the light, the base hull flickering at 4%
    I  it holds a carved lantern (its light, now carried)
    II the hull is whole, still cold
    III the lantern burns warm
    end gone; its candle stays lit

THE BOOKKEEPER (existing; optional cameo)
  Unfiled runs are the one thing not in his ledger. One dry line at most,
  e.g. in the codex: "Not my department. Somebody ought to have made it one."


================================================================================
2. THE PLACE
================================================================================

THE HATCH (menu, October only)
  A way down, separate from the Outsider's hole, so that every player can
  reach the event, including those who have never let him in. It is a
  trapdoor in the menu, with light and three knocks coming up through it.
  Label: THE VIGIL — [V].

THE VIGIL (the event hub)
  A low, cold room under the floor. It is walked like the hub.
  - THE SOUL sits on the lowest landing and moves up one landing for each
    ascension.
  - THREE WAYS UP, each opening with its chapter: to THE PATCH, THE HOUSE
    and THE YARD.
  - THE CANDLES: the worldwide counter, drawn. One candle on the floor for
    every 10 lit worldwide, so the room fills and brightens as the event
    goes on. Each prize has an alcove that lights up when its milestone
    lands.
  - THE STAND: where medals are hung. Walk up to it and choose two.
  - THE WALL: empty until the end, when the soul's run is written on it.
  - THE OUTSIDER stands at the foot of the stairs.

      -- ASCENSION II --

  One profile can light at most 100 candles, and that cap is what keeps the
  worldwide count honest (section 8).

THE ERRAND BEFORE EACH BOSS is my addition. The brief was "kill a boss,
ascend, next quest". The errands give each chapter a beat before its boss
and give the soul something to get back each time. Cutting them leaves a
clean 3 + 1 chain, and the numbers above would be redistributed.

CHAPTERS OPEN BY DATE (recommended):
  Chapter I     1 Oct     the prologue, the vigil and THE PATCH
  Chapter II   11 Oct     THE HOUSE
  Chapter III  21 Oct     THE YARD and the epilogue
  Closes        1 Nov 00:00 UTC
  Why: the whole community moves through the story together, the counter
  rises in steps, each unlock gives a reason to come back, and it gives the
  build (especially the art) ten days per chapter instead of four days for
  all of it. Once a chapter opens it stays open until the event closes, so
  anyone can catch up. The dates come from the server's clock, the same
  authority the daily uses, with the local clock only as a stand-in.

HOW AN AREA IS PLAYED
  - You go in through its way up in the vigil, flying the pilot you have
    selected.
  - It plays like a stage: five waves of the area's own roster, with the
    boss on the fifth.
  - Each area is scaled as a slice of an ordinary run:
      THE PATCH   waves 6–10
      THE HOUSE   waves 11–15
      THE YARD    waves 16–20   (it ends where the soul ended)
  - You go in with a head start: the level-ups a run would have by then,
    picked at the door (THE VAGRANT gets gear drops instead). The exact
    number is a tuning question.
  - An errand's progress is kept between attempts. Dying costs the attempt,
    not the pieces you already have.
  - The boss only comes on wave 5 once that chapter's errand is done. So a
    strong player can finish a whole chapter in one run (errand in waves
    2–4, boss on 5), and anyone else does it in two.
  - Solo only in the first version. Co-op can come later, since new
    enemy types sync through ETYPE on their own.

CHAPTER I — THE PATCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A field under a low orange light. Every gourd in it is a run somebody paused
and never opened again. THE BACKLOG farms them.

AREA RULE — RIPENING
  A gourd left alone ripens: over 15 s its face carves itself in, then it
  splits into a JACK. And the pile weighs on the room: every unbroken gourd
  makes everything else in the room 3% faster.

ENEMIES
  GOURD      Does not move and does not attack. It ripens. Priced like a
             CLUTCH, but you can watch the countdown on its face, and the
             pile rule makes ignoring gourds cost you everywhere at once.
  JACK       A lit lantern. It lobs seeds in an arc and breathes flame at
             short range. The face is the weak point: rounds through it do
             double and put the candle out, and a Jack with no candle
             cannot breathe.
  CREEPER    Throws a vine onto you. While tethered you are slowed 40% and
             reeled in. Shoot the vine or dash through it.
  SCARECROW  Holds perfectly still while your aim is on it, and moves only
             while you aim anywhere else. It hits hard up close. The room
             is built to make you look away.

QUEST 1 — THE REST OF IT
  Given:
    SOUL      "...three of them are mine. the cold ones."
    OUTSIDER  "Three of the gourds up there are not orange. Break them before
               they ripen, or the one who farms them keeps them."
  Task: one COLD GOURD comes up in each of waves 2, 3 and 4. Break it before
  it ripens (the same 15 s). A broken one is kept across attempts. One that
  ripens goes to THE BACKLOG's heap and comes up again on the next attempt.
  Three to finish.
  Done:
    SOUL      "...that's what i was flying."
    [the hull outline gathers round its light]
    OUTSIDER  "An ordinary hull. The one everyone starts in."
  Pays: 10 candles, THE GOURD.

QUEST 2 — ON SALE
  Given:
    SOUL      "...he has the rest. he says he's going to play it."
    OUTSIDER  "He has more than he will ever finish. He is not giving one back
               because you asked."
  Task: THE PATCH, wave 5. Kill THE BACKLOG.

  BOSS — THE BACKLOG
    Who: the hoarder, who buys everything and finishes nothing. It believes
    one day it will get to all of it, and that belief is the whole problem.
    It is a heap that walks: gourds, sealed boxes, and a price sticker on
    everything.
    Gimmick — UNFINISHED:
      - Every attack it starts, it abandons at some point along the way. A
        volley freezes in the air, dimmed and marked paused. A sweep stops
        partway and hangs as a line. A slam leaves its marker on the floor.
        Every paused thing stays where it is. That is the pile.
      - THE BINGE, at 75/50/25%: everything paused resumes at once.
      - COUNTERPLAY: shooting a paused thing resumes it on the spot, so you
        can clear the pile one piece at a time from a safe angle. Anything
        resumed hurts him as much as you, so lure him into a paused volley's
        path and then shoot it.
      - It walks to unbroken gourds and adds them to its heap: 3% heal and a
        little armour each. The area rule carries into the fight.
    Lines:
      entrance   "Oh — careful. Those are mine."
                 "I'll get to it."
                 "It was on sale."
                 "Not now. Later."
                 "I'm saving that one."
      a gourd    "Another one. For later."
      binge      "Fine. FINE. All of it. Now."
      death      "...I was going to play that one."

  Done — ASCENSION I:
    [The heap spills across the patch, and the soul's save rolls out of it.
    The soul rises to the first landing, and it holds a carved lantern.]
    SOUL      "wave nineteen. hull at four percent. that's where i am. that's
               where i've been."
    SOUL      "i can go up now. there's a house."
    OUTSIDER  "He was never going to play it. Nobody plays all of them."
  Pays: 15 candles, ONE DAY.

CHAPTER II — THE HOUSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An empty lobby: a house where every room is a menu somebody left open. The
lights are on a timer, and nobody is left to reset it.

AREA RULE — THE QUIET
  The light closes in while nothing dies, and every kill throws it back out.
  Go about four seconds without a kill and the room goes dark past your hull
  (the existing fog), and the dark keeps closing.

ENEMIES
  SHEET        A sheet over something else. The sheet soaks up the first 40%
               of the damage. Tear it off, and whatever was under it (anything
               from the main roster) is out.
  LURKER       Lives in the dark. It cannot be seen past the light, it moves
               only in the dark, and it strikes from there. The light burns
               it: caught inside it, a lurker flees and takes double damage.
               Keeping the kills coming is what keeps it off you.
  POLTERGEIST  Never attacks directly. It picks up wreckage and bodies and
               throws them.

QUEST 3 — LAST MESSAGE
  Given:
    SOUL      "...someone said something before it stopped. it's in the house."
    OUTSIDER  "The house keeps whatever was said in it last. It is written on
               the underside of the sheets. I would not have looked."
  Task: one MARKED SHEET (edged in cold light, and it runs from you) comes in
  each of waves 2, 3 and 4. Tear each one off; written underneath is one line
  of the lobby's last chat. They're kept across attempts. Three to finish, and
  they read in order:
      ez
      wait
      brb
  Done:
    SOUL      "ez. wait. brb."
    SOUL      "that's him. that's all he said."
    OUTSIDER  "Three words. People have been left with less."
  Pays: 10 candles, SHEET.

QUEST 4 — STILL HERE
  Given:
    SOUL      "...there's someone in the house who says nobody's coming back."
    OUTSIDER  "He has been telling it that since before I found it. He tells
               everyone. He is always here to say it."
  Task: THE HOUSE, wave 5. Kill DEAD GAME.

  BOSS — DEAD GAME
    Who: the commenter who calls the game dead after every update, and who is
    here every single day to say so. It cares too much to leave and is too
    proud to play. It is a ghost shaped like a comment, a speech bubble with a
    face, trailing a thread of replies.
    Gimmick — ONLY SOLID WHILE THE ROOM IS ALIVE:
      - The HUD gets a meter, ONLINE. Every kill raises it (yours, an ally's,
        anything's), and it drains all the time.
      - Above its line, DEAD GAME is solid and can be hurt.
      - Below the line, it is a ghost: it can't be hurt, it drifts, it heals,
        and it says so.
      - Its main move is THEY LEFT: it culls its own adds in a grey wave, to
        starve you of kills. The fight is keeping the room alive: kill its
        adds before it takes them back, and hold some alive for the moment
        you need the meter.
      - It leaves LAST SEEN marks, the grey shapes of players who left. They
        are decoys: harmless, and worth nothing.
      - Last phase: the house goes fully dark, and only kills light it.
    Lines:
      entrance    "Oh. Someone's here. Weird."
                  "Dead game."
                  "Nobody plays this anymore."
                  "It was better before."
                  "Look around. Nobody."
      culling     "They left."
      fading out  "See? Nothing."
                  "Why are you even still here?"
      to the soul "Nobody's coming back for you."
      death       "...still here, though."

  Done — ASCENSION II:
    [The soul rises to the second landing. Its hull is whole now, and it speaks
    in full sentences.]
    SOUL      "He's wrong. Someone said he'd be right back. I'm going to go and
               get him."
    OUTSIDER  "He said 'still here'. I say that."
    OUTSIDER  "I did not like hearing it from him."
  Pays: 15 candles, STILL HERE.

  NOTE: this is close to THE NEXT THING (drafts, 3), where the audience
  drifts away. The two differ in who is doing it: THE NEXT THING never cared,
  and DEAD GAME cares too much to go. Keep its lines about staying, never
  about leaving.

CHAPTER III — THE YARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A graveyard of runs. Each headstone is one run that was quit, with its wave,
its hull and the last thing typed in it. He buries them himself, and never
comes back to any of them.

AREA RULE — RISING
  A grave rises when a body is killed on its plot or when the bell tolls. A
  RISEN climbs out.

ENEMIES
  RISEN        A ghost hull flying a quit run's weapon, chosen at random: a
               stream, a short lance or a swing. The mimic's copied-gun code,
               fed a random build rather than yours. When one dies, it leaves
               its headstone line behind for a moment:
                 "WAVE 12 · 'lag'"
                 "WAVE 7 · 'this is rigged'"
                 "WAVE 22 · 'afk'"
                 "WAVE 3 · 'boring'"
                 "WAVE 31 · 'gtg'"
  GRAVEDIGGER  Digs graves where you have been: open pits that slow you and
               swallow shots. A body killed near it is buried, and rises later.
  BELLRINGER   A stationary bell. It tolls every 6 s, and each toll raises the
               three nearest graves. Put it down first.

QUEST 5 — WHERE HE PUT ME
  Given:
    SOUL      "He put me somewhere up there, with the others."
    OUTSIDER  "There are a great many stones up there. Most of them say the
               same thing."
  Task: in each of waves 2, 3 and 4, a GRAVEDIGGER marked with the soul's cold
  light goes to one particular stone and starts to fill it in, which takes
  20 s. Kill it before it finishes. Each kill is kept across attempts. Three
  to finish, and then the stone is uncovered:
      HERE LIES A RUN
      WAVE 19 · HULL 4%
      "brb"
  Done:
    SOUL      "That's mine."
    SOUL      "He put 'brb' on it. He knew he wasn't coming back when he wrote
               it."
    OUTSIDER  "Most of them say that."
  Pays: 10 candles, SHALLOW GRAVE.

QUEST 6 — GG
  Given:
    SOUL      "He's up there. He's always up there, digging."
    OUTSIDER  "He leaves before he loses. He has never lost. That is not the
               same as winning, but he does not know that."
  Task: THE YARD, wave 5. Kill THE RAGE QUIT.

  BOSS — THE RAGE QUIT
    Who: the sore loser, who closes the tab before a run can end on him and
    blames the lag. Every stone in the yard is one of his runs. Its look is
    a figure with a controller cord for a tail and a cracked screen for a
    face.
    Gimmick — HE QUITS BEFORE HE LOSES:
      - The yard has four GATES at its edges. Each time his health crosses a
        quarter, he bolts for the nearest open gate.
      - If he reaches it: "THAT ONE DIDN'T COUNT." The room jolts back, and
        he comes back in with the health he had at the start of that
        quarter, bringing the graves he raised with him.
      - A gate is shut while someone stands in it. You can hold one with
        your hull. While he is running, his back is to you, so he takes 50%
        more damage: burn the quarter down before he gets there, or body-block
        the gate he is making for.
      - From the second quarter, THE SOUL holds one gate: the one he left it
        through.
      - In the last quarter, when he bolts, the RISEN stop fighting you. They
        walk into the other gates and hold them. Every run he left is
        standing in a door. He cannot leave, and he loses for the first time.
      - Otherwise, his tilt: the lower his health, the faster and wilder he
        gets. He throws the controller (a projectile that bounces across the
        whole yard), mashes (a spray), and charges ("I wasn't even trying").
    Lines:
      entrance    "Oh great. This again."
                  "That one didn't count."
                  "Lag."
                  "I wasn't even trying."
                  "Again."
                  "This game is rigged."
      bolting     "I'm done. I'm DONE."
      restart     "That one didn't count."
      the soul takes its gate:
        SOUL      "You said you'd be right back."
        RAGE QUIT "...who are you?"
        SOUL      "Wave nineteen."
      the risen fill the gates:
        RAGE QUIT "No. No no no. Let me out."
      death       "...gg."

  Done — ASCENSION III:
    [The soul rises to the top landing, and its lantern burns warm.]
    SOUL      "He said it. He finally said it."
    SOUL      "I'm still at nineteen, though. Nobody finished me. He only
               stopped."
    SOUL      "The others up there — not all of them were left, you know. Some
               of them were taken. I could hear them."
    OUTSIDER  "Then there will be more of this."
    OUTSIDER  "It needs finishing first."
  Pays: 20 candles, LAG.

================================================================================
5. MEDALS
================================================================================

Two are worn at a time, chosen at THE STAND in the vigil. They apply to every
run while the event is on: ordinary runs, the BOSS RUSH and the event areas.
They do not apply in dailies or challenges, which are fixed rule sheets that
everyone plays the same. When the event closes they stop working and stay on
the stand as a record.

Every one of them is pilot-agnostic, so THE VAGRANT gets the full value.

  THE GOURD      Anything you leave alive for 15 s ripens: +50% health, and it
                 pays double XP and credits when it dies.
  ONE DAY        XP is not paid as it is earned. It is put aside, then paid all
                 at once, plus half again, when a boss dies.
  SHEET          The dash keeps you intangible 0.35 s longer, and you pass
                 through bodies while it lasts. It recharges 25% slower.
  STILL HERE     Once a run, the killing blow leaves you a ghost for 8 s: you
                 cannot be hurt, and you deal half damage. Kill 10 before it
                 ends and you stand back up at 30% hull. Kill fewer, and the
                 run ends.
  SHALLOW GRAVE  One body in ten you kill climbs back out on your side for 8 s
                 (the HACKER's own ally code). On THE HACKER itself, its bodies
                 last 25% longer instead.
  LAG            One enemy round in six that comes within reach stutters and
                 hangs in the air for a second.
  THE LANTERN    The soul's lantern rides at your shoulder. It shoots what you
                 shoot, weakly, and once a wave it takes a hit for you.

All numbers are first drafts, for tuning in play.


================================================================================
6. THE VIGIL — THE WORLDWIDE COUNT
================================================================================

Every candle anyone lights is added to one number the whole world shares.
The room draws it (one candle for every 10), and prizes unlock at
milestones.

WHO GETS THEM: every profile that has lit at least one candle gets every
prize the vigil reaches, including prizes reached after it stopped playing.
The count is communal, so the prizes are too.

THE PRIZES are one skin line, a lantern going from carved to burning. It
is written once as an effect that gains detail with each tier, the way the
imperial line is (DEV_SKINS, fx 'hallows', tier 1–3), with one more on top.

  MILESTONE   PRIZE
  ---------   -------------------------------------------------------------
     150      THE CARVED     tier 1: the face cut into the hull, unlit
     400      THE LANTERN    tier 2: lit from inside; rounds as sparks
     750      ALL HALLOWS    tier 3: burning; kills come apart as embers
   1,200      THE LOST SOUL  the base hull as a ghost, flickering at 4%

THE SCALE: September's board has 18 names, and every profile can light 100
at most. So 750 needs about eight people to finish the story, and 1,200 about
twelve. The milestones live on the server, so they can be retuned mid-event
without shipping the game.


================================================================================
7. FOR NEXT YEAR (Book II hooks)
================================================================================

Left open on purpose, so that Book II is the next chapter of this rather
than a new event:

  1. "Some of them were taken." Not every run in the yard was left. Some
     were taken, which gives Book II its antagonist: whoever takes runs. That
     is still to be decided, and it leaves room for another person in the
     game's usual way.
  2. "There are more down there." THE PATCH still holds thousands of gourds,
     and the vigil, its candle and the three ways up all stay.
  3. THE OUTSIDER. The soul was left, and he left. Why he walked out of the
     crowd can be the through-line from one book to the next.
  4. START COUNTING NOW. Log real abandoned runs from this update on: a tab
     closed (pagehide) during play records {pilot, wave, date} on the
     profile. It's cheap and invisible, and next October the patch can hold
     the player's OWN lost runs, with their own waves and pilots on them.
     The data needs a year to exist, so it has to start this year.
  5. Book I can come back each October for anyone who missed it, beside
     that year's new book.

================================================================================
9. THE LOOK — a higher animation budget
================================================================================

The brief (user, 27 Sep): this should look better than anything the game has
done so far.

WHERE THE CEILING IS NOW
  Everything is canvas 2D, drawn procedurally, with post passes on the
  compositor (bloom taps, the channel split, grade, vignette, grain). The
  best things in the game are the rites and the kits. They look good through
  drawing effort: every glow, trail and limb is written by hand, per piece,
  in that piece's own art file. Nothing is shared between them, so each new
  piece starts from zero.

THE MOVE: spend the budget on TOOLS first, then on drawing.
  A shared runtime, THE KIT, is built once. Every piece of the event is
  authored against it: the soul, the rooms, the bosses, the enemies and the
  cutscenes. It does what the game cannot do today, and it gives everything
  in the event one look.

WHY LIGHT COMES FIRST
  The whole story is about light. There are the vigil's candles, the soul's
  lantern, THE HOUSE going dark when nothing dies, and a graveyard at night.
  The game has glows but no light: nothing in it is ever lit by something
  else. The single biggest jump in quality is a real light pass.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE KIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LIGHT
  - A light buffer at full resolution. Each frame it is filled
    with the room's ambient colour, the lights are added into it, and it is
    multiplied over the world before the bloom, so lit things bloom and dark
    things sink.
  - Lights have a colour, a radius, a falloff and a flicker. The flicker is
    layered noise, so no two candles breathe alike.
  - SHADOWS: props (stones, furniture, the gourds, the bosses) can be marked
    as occluders, and each light casts soft shadows from them. Candlelight
    throwing long shadows off headstones is the image this event should be
    remembered for.
  - Emitters glow additively on top, so the flame and the room it lights
    read as the same thing.
  - Each area gets a grade: its ambient light, its tint, and how deep its
    blacks go.

CLOTH & ROPE (verlet)
  - Ropes: vines, the RAGE QUIT's cord, DEAD GAME's trailing thread of
    replies, the soul's wisp tail, and cobwebs.
  - Cloth: SHEETs, curtains in THE HOUSE, and the Outsider's coat if he gets
    one. Cloth can TEAR along its constraints, so a SHEET's death is the sheet
    ripping apart.

RIG
  - Bone chains with pose blending, two-bone IK (a hand reaching the rim of
    the hatch, a digger's spade into the ground) and spring-driven secondary
    motion (lag, overshoot, settle).
  - Squash and stretch helpers.
  - This is the machinery the AMALGAM's bones had to hand-roll, made general.

TIMELINE (the cutscene sequencer)
  - Beats on a clock, with eased tweens on anything: the camera (pan, zoom,
    roll, shake), letterbox, fades, and dialogue.
  - The clock is wall-safe (a hidden tab doesn't break a beat) and
    skippable (hold Esc).
  - It is the ARR.T arrival table grown up: the same beats-with-timestamps
    idea, but reusable.
  - In the harness it has a SCRUBBER, so a scene can be dragged to any frame
    and held there while it is being animated. That is the tool that makes a
    higher animation budget workable, rather than replaying a 20-second scene
    to check one beat.

DIALOGUE
  - One presentation, with a voice per speaker:
    - The Outsider: flat, steady text.
    - The soul: lowercase fragments that type unevenly and trail, gaining
      steadiness with each ascension.
    - Boss lines as barks in the world, styled per boss: DEAD GAME as comment
      bubbles, THE RAGE QUIT as a chat line in capitals, THE BACKLOG with
      price stickers.

PARTICLES
  - Pooled emitters: embers, candle smoke that curls, dust motes turning in
    the light, bats, leaves, wax drips, grave dirt.

BAKED LAYERS
  - Each area's floor and fixed props can be as detailed as the design
    wants, because they are rendered once into tiles rather than redrawn
    every frame.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE THE BUDGET GOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  S — the hero moments. The most time per second on screen.
      The soul: 6 states, 3 ascensions, and the wave-20 exit. The prologue
      knock. The vigil and its candles. Three boss entrances and three
      deaths. The RAGE QUIT's gates filling. The epilogue.
  A — the bosses in the fight (every attack's windup, telegraph, release and
      recovery; phase changes). The areas and their rules: faces carving in
      as the gourds ripen, THE QUIET closing in, graves rising. The stand.
  B — ten enemies, each with idle, move, attack, hurt and death. Every death
      is bespoke: a gourd splits, a sheet tears, a lurker burns away in the
      light, a risen crumbles into its epitaph, a bell cracks. Plus the prize
      skins and the medal icons.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PERFORMANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Not a constraint (user, 27 Sep: "don't worry about performance
  constraints"). There is no frame budget and nothing to trim. The light pass
  runs at full resolution, every light casts shadows, and cloth, particles
  and baked layers are as rich as the look wants.
  The one rule that stays is about design, not frames: THE HOUSE's darkness
  is a gameplay rule, so it keeps the existing fog underneath the lighting,
  and the light pass is what makes it beautiful.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IT GETS MADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The same pipeline as the SUPERUSER and OVERDRIVE art, with a better base:
  - hl-base.js: the game shims plus THE KIT. It is the harness base, as
    su-base.js is, but the kit inside it is the same code that ships, so what
    is designed is what runs.
  - One design file per piece (a dc.html), each with its own sim and art file
    and a scrubber for its timeline.
  - Hero moments get a TIMING SHEET first: beats with timestamps, drafted by
    me and reshaped by the user before any drawing.
  - I build the logic under placeholder hooks, the user designs the art on the
    kit, and the whole art file is lifted in, as su-art.js and od-art.js
    are.
